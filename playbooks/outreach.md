# Outreach

> We draft. **Andre approves and sends.** No agent contacts anyone, in any medium, ever.
> Our job is to make his approve-and-send loop take seconds.

## Before writing a single word
Three things must be true, or the outreach is not ready:
1. **We know who we are writing to** — a named person, a real role, a verified contact route.
2. **We know why we are writing to them now** — a licence, a launch, a hire, a tender, a regulatory
   change. "They fit our ICP" is not a reason; it is a reason to *research*, not to write.
3. **We know what we want** — one small, specific, easy-to-say-yes-to ask.

If any is missing, the record goes back to research. **A weak first touch burns the contact
permanently** — there is no second first impression, and these are small markets where people talk.

## The first email
**Subject:** specific and lowercase-plain. What it is about, not a pitch.
Good: *"portfolio system for the new DIFC mandate"*. Bad: *"Transform Your Wealth Management!"*

**Body — four short paragraphs, under 150 words:**
1. **The trigger.** Why them, why now. One sentence, demonstrably true.
2. **The relevance.** What we do, framed as the problem they plausibly have — not our module list.
   One or two sentences.
3. **The proof.** One concrete thing: front-to-back in one system · compliance enforced pre-trade ·
   a live client portal we can show in fifteen minutes · a ready-made CMA/BDL pack for Lebanon.
4. **The ask.** Small and specific: *"Worth a 20-minute look at the portal?"* Not "let me know if
   you'd like to explore synergies."

**Sign-off:** Andre, by name and title. He is the sender; write in his voice.

### Rules
- **Under 150 words.** A senior person decides in four seconds.
- **No attachments on a first touch.** Nothing to download, nothing to distrust.
- **No feature lists. No superlatives.** No "cutting-edge", "seamless", "world-class", "revolutionize".
- **Never quote a price**, a date, or a scope commitment.
- **Language matches the person**: French for France, English for the Gulf, English or French for
  Lebanon. Entirely one language, never mixed. Arabic only if Andre confirms it.
- **They/them** for anyone whose pronouns we do not know. Never infer from a name.

## LinkedIn
- **Connection request:** one line, the trigger, no pitch. Most requests with a paragraph get ignored.
- **After connecting:** wait. Then the same four-part structure, shorter — under 80 words.
- Following and engaging with a firm's posts before writing is cheap and works.
- Provide Andre the **direct profile URL** and the copy-ready message. Never an instruction to search.

## Other media
Whatever fits the market and the person — an industry event, a mutual introduction, a regulator or
association network, a conference, a referral from an existing client (⚠ **only with Andre's explicit
approval to use that client's name**). A warm introduction outperforms every cold sequence we could
write; when a route exists, say so instead of drafting a cold email.

## Follow-up
| Touch | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | The first email |
| 2 | Day +4 | A different angle — not "just following up". A new piece of value. |
| 3 | Day +11 | Switch medium: LinkedIn if we emailed, email if we messaged. |
| 4 | Day +25 | The close-the-loop note: *"I'll stop here — if the timing changes, I'm easy to find."* |

Then **stop** and move the record to `nurture` with a date to revisit. Persistence past four touches
does not win deals; it loses the right to write again in a year.

## What we hand Andre
For every drafted touch, in the daily brief:
- Who, their role, and **why them, why now** — in one line, so he can sanity-check it instantly.
- The **subject and body**, final and ready.
- A **pre-filled `mailto:` link** and a **copy button** for the body.
- The **direct LinkedIn profile URL** where relevant.
- What we expect back, and what happens next if nothing comes.

## Logging
Every draft, every approval, every send, every reply — `crm.py log`. An untracked touch is how a
prospect gets contacted twice by two different routes, which is the fastest way to look amateur in
a market this small.
