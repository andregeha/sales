## 1. UAE

### 1.1 Market structure — four regulators, four registers, and they do not overlap
The UAE is genuinely multi-jurisdictional; a firm can be licensed onshore, in DIFC, or in ADGM, and
these are different legal systems with different regulators. Conflating them is a known failure
mode.

| Regulator | Scope | Public register? | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SCA** (Securities and Commodities Authority) — now operating as the UAE's federal Capital Market Authority | Onshore (mainland) UAE securities business: brokers, investment managers, fund/portfolio managers, financial consultants | **Yes** — Open Data "Licensed Companies" | https://www.sca.gov.ae/en/open-data/licensed-companies.aspx (mirrored at https://www.uaecma.gov.ae/en/open-data/licensed-companies.aspx) |
| **DFSA** (Dubai Financial Services Authority) | DIFC-based financial firms only (a distinct common-law jurisdiction inside Dubai) | **Yes** — Public Register of Authorised Firms | https://www.dfsa.ae/public-register/firms |
| **FSRA** (Financial Services Regulatory Authority) | ADGM-based financial firms only (Abu Dhabi's equivalent free zone) | **Yes** — Public Register | https://www.adgm.com/public-registers/fsra |
| **CBUAE** (Central Bank of the UAE) | Onshore banks, finance companies, insurers (since Federal Decree-Law No. 6/2025, a much broader remit) | **Yes** — CB Register (PDF, published periodically) | https://www.centralbank.ae/en/licensing/ (register PDFs e.g. https://www.centralbank.ae/media/1z4gwagt/cb-register-june-2025.pdf) |

Scale, dated:
- **DFSA/DIFC**: the DFSA supervises **979 Authorised Firms** in DIFC (DFSA website, search-confirmed 2026-09-22, no explicit as-of date on the figure — treat as current-ish rather than pinned). Separately, DIFC's own 2025 annual results report **1,052 regulated companies**, including **557 wealth & asset management firms**, **102 hedge funds**, **135 insurance/reinsurance companies**, **70 brokerage firms**, and regional headquarters for **290+ banks and capital-markets institutions** (DIFC 2025 annual results, reported by The National and AGBI, 2026-02-05/06). Note the two counts (979 vs 1,052) come from different DFSA/DIFC publications taken at different times — **do not average them**, cite whichever the underlying source uses.
- **ADGM/FSRA**: ADGM had **almost 14,000 active licences** across all categories in 2026 per one source, and separately **crossed 12,000 active licences at end-2025** (a ~30% annual increase) per another — these figures are **not consistent with each other** and likely measure different things (all commercial licences vs. financial-services-only); **unknown** which is the precise FSRA-regulated-firm count as of today. Use the FSRA public register itself to get an authoritative, filterable count rather than either secondary figure.
- **SCA (onshore)**: companies licensed for **portfolio management and investment fund activities** rose from 18 (Jan 2023) to **33** (2024), an 83% increase (SCA/uaecma.gov.ae press release, 2024-08-27, and economymiddleeast.com, 2024). This is a small, countable, high-value population — every one of the 33 is a qualified lead.
- **CBUAE (onshore banks)**: **61–62 licensed banks** (24–25 national, ~37 foreign branches, 9 Islamic) as of Jan–Mar 2026, per CBUAE register summaries (secondary sources, 2026). Confirm exact current count from the CBUAE register PDF before using in materials.

**Unknown**: an authoritative, single, current count of DIFC- or ADGM-licensed **asset/fund managers and private banks specifically** (as opposed to "wealth & asset management" broadly, which is a wider category that includes advisory and family-office-adjacent firms). Get this by filtering the DFSA/FSRA public registers by activity, not from press summaries.

### 1.2 Where the family-office population actually is
Family offices are the segment registers cannot see, structurally:
- **DIFC**: since the **Family Arrangements Regulations**, a qualifying **single-family office** (family net global assets ≥ **US$50m**) needs **no DFSA licence and no DNFBP registration** — it is invisible to the DFSA public register by design (Neo Legal / Hourani & Partners / Kayrouz & Associates, 2025–2026 summaries of the regime). Only **multi-family offices serving multiple families for a fee** must be DFSA-authorised — those *do* show up on the DFSA register, and are the one legitimately searchable subset.
- **ADGM**: a **single family office** is a licensable-but-largely-exempt "controlled activity" with a family net-asset gate of **US$10m** (ADGM Commercial Licensing Regulations, 2025/2026 rules, per Crimson Legal / Navira / Savvy Setup summaries, 2026) — again mostly outside financial-services licensing.
- **Scale signals, not a register**: DIFC reported (2025 annual results, 2026-02-05/06) **1,289 family-related entities** and **1,115–1,250 family foundations** (figures vary slightly by source/date), family-related entities up **61%** and foundations up **66%** year-on-year; **over 140 single family offices** registered in DIFC by early 2026; **the top ~120 families** operating out of DIFC collectively manage **over US$1.2 trillion**. ADGM reports **100+ single family offices** and **12,000+ active licences** overall with AUM up ~36% in 2025 (intuitconsultancy.com, maxicapitalist.com, 2026). DIFC is cited as the **world's second-largest family office hub** (same source set, 2026).
- **How to actually find them, practically, ranked by usability**:
  1. **DIFC/ADGM company-register name searches** (the general company registers, not the financial-services register) will show entities with "family office" or "foundation" in the name or structure, but do not expose beneficial-ownership/AUM detail publicly — usable for a name list, not a qualification list.
  2. **Foundation filings** (DIFC/ADGM foundations regime) are a proxy signal — a new foundation registration is often a family-office-adjacent event, but the register does not label "this is a family office."
  3. **Industry events, forums and summits** (e.g., Gulf/Saudi/UAE family office summits, wealth conferences) and their speaker/attendee/sponsor lists are, pragmatically, the **best usable lead source** for named principals and CIOs — better than any register.
  4. **Press and league-table coverage** (Forbes/Bloomberg/CFA Institute/regional business press on "family offices moving to Dubai") surfaces named families opportunistically but is not systematic.
  5. **Industry bodies**: unlike France's AFFO (below), **no dedicated UAE/GCC family-office association with a public member directory was found** in this research — flag as **unknown / possibly does not exist as a usable public directory**; worth a follow-up check before the lead-sourcing routine is built.

### 1.3 Market dynamics
- **Regulatory consolidation, not fragmentation, is the live story**: **Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025** (effective 2025-09-16) rewrites the Central Bank Law, folding payment providers, insurers, **open finance**, and virtual-asset payment services under one CBUAE framework, and creates a **new licensing requirement for "enabling technology providers."** Firms newly in scope have **until 2025-09-16 + 12 months = 2026-09-16 to regularise** (Gibson Dunn, White & Case, Addleshaw Goddard, GLA & Co, 2025) — that compliance deadline has just passed (six days before this brief). Anyone still scrambling to comply is a live trigger.
- **Explosive, well-documented growth** in DIFC and ADGM family-office and wealth-management registrations (see 1.2) — this is a segment that is actively professionalising, which is exactly the moment a family office first buys a real system instead of spreadsheets.
- **SCA onshore portfolio-management/fund licensing nearly doubled in a year** (18→33, 2023→2024) — a small but fast-growing, fully enumerable population.

### 1.4 Buying triggers to watch
New DFSA/FSRA/SCA licence grant · a family office converting from single- to multi-family (triggers DFSA/FSRA authorisation) · a new foundation registration followed by hires · the **CBUAE Decree-Law 6/2025 compliance deadline** (2026-09-16, just passed) forcing newly-in-scope firms to formalise systems and controls · a bank or insurer spinning out its DIFC/ADGM wealth arm · a new CIO/COO/Head of Ops hire announced in regional business press · an incumbent vendor being acquired (see France/global section on Multifonds, 1.4 in France below — relevant UAE-wide too since many UAE fund administrators/managers run global platforms).

### 1.5 Practical entry considerations
OFS already has a **Dubai office** (trading as GAIA PORTFOLIO SOFTWARE L.L.C. — per `knowledge/company/ofs-profile.md`; company name and PRO-agent details are internal-only, never client-facing). English is the working language for DIFC/ADGM and for onshore capital-markets business; Arabic matters for onshore government-adjacent and retail-facing work but is not a blocker for the priority segments here. No blanket local-partner requirement was found for DIFC/ADGM financial firms buying software (unlike Saudi's local-content regime, 2.5 below) — **unknown** whether SCA-licensed onshore firms have a distinct procurement/local-content expectation; worth confirming before an onshore SCA-segment push.

## 2. Saudi Arabia

### 2.1 Market structure
Saudi has **no DIFC/ADGM-style separate financial free zone** — CMA and SAMA regulate directly, nationwide. This is a structural contrast with the UAE worth remembering when comparing "how many regulators" across markets.

| Regulator | Scope | Public register? | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CMA** (Capital Market Authority) | Capital Market Institutions (CMIs): asset managers, brokers, advisors, custodians, fund managers | **Yes** — Authorised Persons / Capital Market Institutions list | https://cma.org.sa/en/Market/AuthorisedPersons/Pages/default.aspx |
| **SAMA** (Saudi Central Bank) | Banks, licensed finance companies, insurers, fintechs (regulatory sandbox) | **Yes** — Licensed Entities pages | https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-US/supervision/licenseentities/pages/default.aspx (permitted fintechs: https://sama.gov.sa/en-US/Regulatory%20Sandbox/pages/permitted-fintechs.aspx) |

Scale, dated:
- **CMIs**: **188 CMIs** listed on the CMA website as of **February 2025** (per secondary legal-industry summary citing CMA data). By **end-2025**, the **total number of licensed financial institutions reached 215** following 32 new licences issued during the year (Arab News / Economy Middle East, reporting CMA data, dated 2025/2026) — a fast-growing, fully enumerable population, similar in character to UAE's SCA list.
- **Banks (SAMA)**: **39 licensed banks** — 11 local banks, 24 foreign bank branches, 4 digital banks (Wikipedia "List of banks in Saudi Arabia," cross-checked against SAMA-sourced press, 2025/2026 — treat the split as approximate).
- **Licensed finance companies (SAMA)**: **71** as of **April 2026** (SAMA press release re: "Modern Integrated Solutions Financing" BNPL licence, 2026-04). These are consumer/SME finance companies, not asset managers — adjacent, not a priority segment, but relevant if OFS ever positions for lending/finance-company back-office.
- **AUM**: Saudi capital-market AUM **exceeded US$320bn (SAR ~1.2 trillion) in 2025**, up 18% year-on-year (Arab News, Economy Middle East, citing CMA, dated 2025/2026); CMI AUM reached **SAR 700bn by end-2024**, growing >25% annually (secondary source, 2025). Private real-estate funds +53%, money-market funds +57%, debt fund AUM +44%, private equity +31% — broad-based growth across almost every fund type CMIs manage, which is exactly the operational complexity Gaia's modules map onto (fund administration, NAV, multi-asset).

### 2.2 Where the family-office population actually is
- Saudi is the one market in this set with an **explicit family-office licensing lane**: **MISA** (Ministry of Investment) introduced a **dedicated family office licence in 2021**; as of the most recent estimate found, **about 45% of Saudi family offices now operate under formal regulatory structures** (praxisrock.com / bridge-connect.com, 2025 — this is an estimate from an industry advisory source, not a regulator-published count; treat as **indicative, not authoritative**).
- Saudi family offices are estimated to manage **US$400–500bn in assets as of 2024** (bridge-connect.com, 2025), with the asset-management side of that regulated by CMA (fund/portfolio management) while MISA/Ministry of Commerce govern the corporate wrapper (holding company, LLC, JSC).
- **Practical identification path**: (1) the **MISA licence process** is the closest thing to a register, but MISA does not appear to publish a public, filterable list of family-office licensees (only general "Investor Guide" material was found) — **unknown** whether a searchable MISA family-office register exists; needs a direct check of misa.gov.sa before assuming one does. (2) Named family offices otherwise surface through **press coverage, praxis/industry "verified firm" directories (private, commercial — e.g. praxisrock.com's list), and dedicated events** such as the Saudi Family Office Summit (saudifamilyofficesummit.com) — same pattern as the UAE: **events and curated commercial directories beat any register.**

### 2.3 Market dynamics
- **Vision 2030 is actively engineering asset-management growth** — CMA's 2024–2026 strategic plan explicitly targets sector development and diversification (Vision2030.ai, King & Spalding, 2025). New **Investment Funds Regulations (2025)** were issued by CMA (cma.gov.sa PDF, 2025) — regulatory refreshes like this are a direct trigger for compliance/reporting system reviews at CMIs.
- **The Kingdom's asset-management industry is forecast to exceed US$400bn AUM by 2026 and become the largest in the GCC** (secondary market-research source, 2025/2026) — the fastest-growing of the four markets in this brief by most measures found.
- **New Investment Law (effective 2025-02-12)** replaced the old per-activity foreign-investment licensing system with a single **Investment Registration Certificate** (setupinsaudi.com / equimerger.com, 2025) — a genuine ease-of-entry improvement for OFS itself, separate from client-side triggers.

### 2.4 Buying triggers to watch
A new CMI licence granted (32 issued in 2025 alone — each one a qualified lead the day it appears) · a fund launch into a fast-growing fund type (real estate, money-market, private equity — all growing >25%/yr) · a family office converting from informal to a MISA-licensed structure · a new CIO/Head of Operations hire at a CMI or bank · CMA's ongoing Investment Funds Regulations refresh forcing reporting/compliance rebuilds · an incumbent global platform vendor requiring local-content-incompatible delivery (see 2.5) creating an opening for a vendor with an actual Riyadh office.

### 2.5 Practical entry considerations — the one market with real local-content friction
- OFS already has a **Riyadh office** (per `knowledge/company/ofs-profile.md`) — this is a genuine differentiator, not a nice-to-have, because:
- Saudi's **Local Content and Government Procurement Authority (LCGPA)** enforces a **30% minimum local-content requirement** on government/state-owned-entity IT and consulting tenders, phased in and weighted into financial evaluation (Saudi Compliance Institute, DGA Group, 2025). The broader **Economic Participation Program (EPP)** requires bidders on major government/SOE contracts to commit to Saudi employment, local sourcing, technology transfer and — notably — **establishing a regional headquarters** in sectors now extending to ICT (DGA Group, 2025).
- **Practical read**: for government-adjacent or SOE-linked buyers (sovereign-linked asset managers, state-backed banks), a **local presence and a local-content story are close to mandatory**, not optional — OFS's Riyadh office should be foregrounded in any Saudi pursuit, and the local-content narrative should be prepared before, not during, a procurement process.
- Language: Arabic matters more here in procurement documentation than in DIFC/ADGM; English is standard for CMI/technical engagement but **unknown** whether Arabic-language contractual/compliance material is a hard requirement for private (non-government) CMIs — confirm case by case.

## 3. Lebanon

### 3.1 Market structure — and a banking sector in the middle of a legally-forced restructuring
| Regulator | Scope | Public register? | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CMA Lebanon** (Capital Markets Authority, est. under Law No. 161/2011) | Non-bank capital-markets intermediaries, and securities-business activities of banks | **Yes, but thin** — an "Approved Institutions" webpage listing licensed entities by name; **not** a structured/downloadable open-data register like AMF's GECO or SCA's open data | https://www.cma.gov.lb/approved-institutions/ |
| **Banque du Liban (BDL)** — the central bank, via its Banking Control Commission | Banks and "financial institutions" (BDL's own regulatory category, distinct from banks — includes leasing, factoring, microfinance, guarantee entities) | **Yes** — two separate static lists (banks; financial institutions), published as PDFs | Banks: https://www.banqueduliban.gov.lb/CB%20Com/Laws%20And%20Regulations/Institutions%20supervised%20by%20BDL/Banklist_en%C2%A73_15.pdf · Financial institutions: https://www.bdl.gov.lb/CB%20Com/Laws%20And%20Regulations/Institutions%20supervised%20by%20BDL/Financial_Institutions_en%C2%A73_16.pdf · Index: https://www.bdl.gov.lb/institutions.php |

Scale, dated: **as of 2022, ~61 banks operated in Lebanon, of which 46 were commercial banks** (secondary source citing IMF-era audit context, 2022) — this predates the 2025 legal restructuring below and the branch count has since fallen **40% over the past seven years** (as of **2026-06**, per UPI/Yahoo reporting, 2026-09-08). **Unknown**: the current, post-restructuring count of banks still operating as going concerns — this is genuinely in flux and any number quoted without a 2026 date should be treated as stale. Get the live count from the BDL list above, not from this brief.

### 3.2 Where the family-office population actually is
Lebanon has **no dedicated family-office regulatory regime and no register** — family offices here are wholly informal or embedded inside private-banking relationships. Some Lebanese banks market an internal "family office" service line (a bank department, not an independent regulated entity) — that is a bank client-service product, not a separate addressable buyer, and should not be double-counted as a prospect. Practical identification: press coverage of prominent Lebanese/diaspora family wealth, regional wealth-management conference attendance, and referral through the Lebanese banking/private-banking community are the only realistic paths — **no usable directory was found**, flag as **unknown/no register exists**.

### 3.3 Market dynamics — the single biggest regulatory trigger in this entire brief
Lebanon's banking sector is undergoing a **legally mandated restructuring**, not a normal-times evolution:
- **2025-04-24**: Lebanese Parliament passed a law **lifting banking secrecy**, ending a regime dating to 1956, under explicit IMF pressure — giving BDL and the Banking Control Commission access to banking data they did not previously have (The National, TIMEP, 2025).
- **Law No. 23 of 2025** ("Reforming and Reorganizing the State of the Banks in Lebanon," passed **2025-07**) creates a **bank-resolution framework**: every bank must be assessed for viability and is headed toward **liquidation, recapitalisation, or merger** (blominvestbank brief, TIMEP, Oxford Law Blogs, 2025–2026).
- A further **"financial gap" law** (draft released **2025-12**) addresses an **~US$80bn hole** from central-bank/government borrowing and sets terms for repaying frozen deposits (Wikipedia "Financial gap law (Lebanon)," TIMEP, 2025-12).
- **What this means commercially**: every surviving or recapitalised Lebanese bank will need to **re-prove its numbers, its books, and its reporting** to a regulator that can now see everything, under a resolution process with hard viability tests. This is about as strong a system-replacement trigger as a vertical software vendor ever gets — but timing risk is real: some counterparties are frozen, being merged, or wound down, not budgeting for new software. **Qualify hard before pursuing**: is this bank a survivor-track institution or a resolution candidate?
- OFS already carries a **ready-made CMA/BDL regulatory pack** (`knowledge/product/gaia-current.md`, confirmed 2026-09-22) — this is a real, usable differentiator specifically because compliance requirements here are unusually well understood and already built, at the exact moment the regulator's actual oversight capacity (post-secrecy-lift) is increasing.

### 3.4 Buying triggers to watch
A bank publicly confirmed as **viable/recapitalising** (survivor-track, as opposed to resolution/liquidation-track) under Law 23/2025 · a bank merger completing (the merged entity needs one system, not two) · BDL/Banking Control Commission issuing new reporting templates under the post-secrecy-lift regime · a financial institution (leasing/factoring/microfinance) professionalising ahead of CMA Lebanon licensing scrutiny · any Lebanese institution's public commitment to IMF-linked reform milestones (each milestone tends to create a compliance-system deadline).

### 3.5 Practical entry considerations
OFS's **Beirut office (OFS Liban SAL)** is the contracting entity for Lebanese clients (`knowledge/company/ofs-profile.md`) — home-market advantage here is total: French/Arabic/English trilingual market, deep existing relationships (OFS already serves Bank Audi and BLOM per `accounts/`), and the CMA/BDL pack already built. The constraint is not access but **counterparty solvency and restructuring-stage risk** — the qualifying question for any Lebanese prospect is "which side of the resolution process are they on," not "do they need a system."

## 4. France

### 4.1 Market structure
| Regulator | Scope | Public register? | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AMF** (Autorité des marchés financiers) | Asset management companies (sociétés de gestion de portefeuille), funds | **Yes** — GECO (launched **2025-02**, replacing/built on the ROSA data referential) with an open-data extract on data.gouv.fr | https://geco.amf-france.org/ · list of authorised management companies: https://geco.amf-france.org/liste-des-societes-de-gestion-agrees-francaise |
| **ACPR** (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, part of Banque de France) | Banks, insurers, most investment firms and finance companies (jointly with AMF for investment firms other than portfolio managers) | **Yes** — REGAFI, a public register updated daily | https://www.regafi.fr/ (advanced search: https://www.regafi.fr/spip.php?page=advancedsearch) |

Scale, dated:
- **Sociétés de gestion**: **700 authorised at end-2023**, slipping to **~697 by late September 2024** — withdrawals slightly outpacing new authorisations (AMF publications / GECO-based reporting, 2024–2025). This is a **mature, slowly consolidating population**, not a growth market in headcount terms — but AUM concentration continues (Amundi alone: **€2.38 trillion AUM in 2025**, Europe's largest, Wikipedia/company reporting, 2025).
- **Banks/investment firms under ACPR**: one secondary source states ACPR supervises **"approximately 720 banks and 760 insurance companies"** — **treat this figure with caution**; it was not corroborated by an ACPR-published headline number in this research and 720 "banks" is high relative to commonly cited French bank counts, so it may include EU-passported branches/agents rather than only French-chartered banks. Separately, as of **2025-09-01**, the ECB's **Single Supervisory Mechanism listed 12 significant French banking groups** directly, plus **93 French "less significant institutions"** supervised via the SSM/ACPR framework (ECB SSM list, cited 2025). **Unknown**: the precise, current REGAFI headline count of French-licensed investment firms and finance companies specifically (as distinct from banks/insurers) — pull live from regafi.fr rather than relying on any number in this brief.

### 4.2 Where the family-office population actually is — France is the one market with a usable association
Unlike the other three markets, France has a **long-established, named industry association with a real membership**: **AFFO (Association Française du Family Office)**, founded **2001**, with **100+ member structures** spanning single-family offices, multi-family offices and family-office professionals, led (as of **early 2025**) by president **Charles-Henri Bujard** (affo.fr, 2025). AFFO publishes an annual **AFFO–EY Baromètre** (the 2025 edition surveyed **585 families**, Feb–Apr 2025, technology the top allocation theme at 63% — clubpatrimoine.com, 2025). **This is the single best family-office lead surface in any of the four markets** — a real membership association, not a proxy signal — though it is a professional body, not a regulator, and it will not hand over its member list; engagement (events, sponsorship, the Baromètre panel) is the realistic access path, not a scrape.
- French multi-family offices that manage third-party assets **by way of business** will separately need **AMF authorisation as a société de gestion de portefeuille** (or fall under the lighter conseiller en investissements financiers regime) — so a subset of the AFFO population is also independently visible on GECO. Cross-referencing AFFO membership against GECO authorisation status is a viable, if labour-intensive, qualification method.

### 4.3 Market dynamics
- **DORA (EU Digital Operational Resilience Act) has applied since 2025-01-17** to UCITS/AIFM management companies, investment firms and market infrastructures in France, enforced jointly by AMF and ACPR; the AMF now **requires management companies to document their digital operational resilience arrangements** in their programme of activity (AMF, 2025). This is a genuine, dated, ongoing compliance-and-systems trigger across the entire French sociétés-de-gestion population — a live reason for a smaller/mid-tier manager to re-examine whether its current stack (often spreadsheets plus a legacy PMS) can produce what DORA now demands.
- **Slow structural consolidation** in the sociétés-de-gestion population (700→697, 2023→2024) means the buyer population is not growing in number, but **AUM concentration and complexity (multi-asset, private markets) keeps rising** — consistent with Gaia's front-to-back, multi-asset pitch being more relevant to survivors, not less.
- **Vendor landscape signal (relevant across all four markets, but sourced here)**: **Temenos sold Multifonds** (fund administration/transfer-agency software, $10tn+ administered) to **Montagu Private Equity** for **~US$400m**, announced **2025-02-06**, closed Q2 2025 (Temenos press release, FinTech Futures, Private Equity Wire, 2025). A large incumbent vendor changing hands is precisely the kind of "vendor exiting/being acquired" trigger flagged in the brief — worth watching Multifonds' installed base (globally, not France-specific) for post-acquisition dissatisfaction or repositioning that creates displacement opportunities.

### 4.4 Buying triggers to watch
An AMF authorisation withdrawal followed by a successor entity being created (succession/reorganisation) · a société de gestion publicly addressing DORA documentation gaps · a family office professionalising into AMF-authorised MFO status · a new CIO/COO hire at a mid-tier asset manager reported in trade press · continued fallout from the Multifonds sale (client repositioning, support changes) · AFFO Baromètre-flagged shifts in family-office allocation (e.g. the 2025 tilt to technology/private markets) signalling operational complexity outgrowing spreadsheets.

### 4.5 Practical entry considerations
OFS is headquartered in **Paris (5 rue Lincoln, 75008)** — this is the home market, no entry barrier. Per `CLAUDE.md`, **all French-speaking-account material must be wholly in French, not mixed** (Dassault/GIMD and AGPM are the confirmed precedents). Procurement norms for French financial institutions typically involve **formal RFP/tender processes** for anything beyond small managers — expect longer, more structured sales cycles than in the Gulf markets. No local-content-style restriction applies (contrast with Saudi, 2.5).

## 5. Where the density is — recommendation

Given limited effort, work in this order:

1. **Saudi Arabia, asset & fund managers (CMIs)** — the strongest combination. A **fully enumerable, small (215 as of end-2025), fast-growing (32 new licences in 2025 alone) population**, sitting inside a market growing AUM >18%/yr with an active regulator issuing new fund regulations, **and** OFS already has a Riyadh office to answer the local-content/local-presence expectation that would otherwise block a foreign vendor. Every new CMA licence is a qualified, dated lead the day it is granted — this is the closest thing to a lead machine in this whole map.
2. **Lebanon, private/investment banks** — the highest-**intensity** trigger in the set (a legally mandated bank resolution process is actively separating survivors from resolution candidates), combined with total home-market advantage (Beirut office, existing bank clients, a pre-built CMA/BDL compliance pack). The catch is qualification risk — effort must go first into determining which institutions are survivor-track, since resolution-track banks are not real buyers regardless of need.
3. **UAE, family offices/MFOs in DIFC** — the best-documented growth story in the brief (family-related entities +61%, foundations +66% in 2025, 140+ single-family offices, $1.2tn managed by the top ~120 families) and a live registration event (SFO→MFO conversion, or new foundation) that is genuinely observable, even though most of the underlying population is unregistered by design. Best worked through the DFSA register (for the MFO subset that must be authorised) plus event/press-based sourcing for the rest.
4. **France, asset & fund managers** — lower urgency (a flat-to-shrinking headcount of ~700 sociétés de gestion) but a real, dated, ongoing compliance trigger (DORA, live since Jan 2025) plus home-market advantage. Treat as a **steady, lower-intensity lane** worked in parallel, not a sprint.

**Lower priority, not zero**: UAE onshore (SCA) portfolio managers/fund managers — small (33 firms) but real and enumerable, worth a light-touch pass; French/GCC private banks generally — the segment is real everywhere but harder to reach through a register (banks buy rarely and via RFP, not off a licence list); Saudi/UAE family offices outside DIFC/ADGM structures — real money, structurally invisible, best reached through events/associations rather than research effort.

**Do not chase**: Lebanese "financial institutions" (BDL's leasing/factoring/microfinance category) and Saudi SAMA-licensed consumer finance companies — adjacent, not priority segments, and not what Gaia is positioned for.
